PE-22-28
the honest unknown

A seven-amino-acid peptide fragment with a Wikipedia page, one key preclinical paper, and almost nothing else verifiable. Vendors sell big stories about it — most of which evaporate on inspection. This page is about what little is actually known, and how to think about compounds like it.

Quick read

PE-22-28 (Gly-Val-Ser-Trp-Gly-Leu-Arg) is a heptapeptide fragment of spadin, itself derived from the sortilin propeptide, studied as a TREK-1 potassium-channel blocker in preclinical mood research. The verifiable record is thin: a 2017 pharmacology paper, a Wikipedia entry, chemical identifiers — and no human trials. Around that thin core orbits a fog of vendor marketing, including sellers who misidentify it as entirely different peptides. The honest story: an obscure research tool with an obscured information environment.

01What is verifiable

What little is verifiable

Most peptide pages start with hype and walk it back. This one starts with everything that can actually be confirmed — it's a short list, and that's the point.

TREK-1 blocker in preclinical research · no human trials

Verifiable: the chemistry

Sequence GVSWGLR, seven amino acids, CAS 1801959-12-5, molecular weight ~789 Da. These identifiers are checkable — the firmest ground this compound has.

Verifiable: the lineage

A shortened fragment of spadin, derived from the sortilin propeptide, described in a 2017 pharmacology paper as a TREK-1 channel blocker with antidepressant-like effects in animals.

Verifiable: the absence

No human trials. No approval. No established safety profile. Everything beyond the chemistry and the preclinical paper is marketing or speculation.

Plain English

The verified file fits in a paragraph: a 7-bead fragment of a natural peptide, studied in animals for mood-related brain effects, never tested in people. Hold every other claim about PE-22-28 up to that paragraph — most won't survive.

02Origin

The thin origin story

PE-22-28's history isn't a saga — it's a footnote that escaped into commerce. Here's the whole thing, honestly told.

From a 2017 paper to a fog of marketing in under a decade.
Spadin came first

Spadin — the parent peptide — was studied as a naturally occurring TREK-1 blocker with fast-acting antidepressant-like effects in animals. PE-22-28 is a trimmed-down version chasing the same activity in a smaller package.

One paper, then commerce

The 2017 paper showed shortened analogs kept TREK-1 blocking activity with better stability. Then vendors moved faster than science — the compound entered catalogs long before any follow-up research.

Why obscurity matters

With famous peptides, hype at least orbits real data. With obscure ones, there's no data gravity — marketing copy drifts wherever sellers want, unchecked by anything.

Plain English

The origin story is a lab notebook entry that became a product listing. A real 2017 finding, genuinely interesting — then a commercial afterlife with no further science attached. Most compounds never get even the paper; this one got the paper and skipped everything after.

04Evidence audit

Evidence audit

An honest audit of every claim tier around PE-22-28 — verified, plausible, and invented, sorted into separate piles.

Verified

Sequence, identifiers, spadin lineage, TREK-1 blocking in the 2017 paper, no human trials. This pile is small and solid.

Plausible but unproven

Antidepressant-like and neurogenic effects in animals. Worth researching; worth nothing as a human claim. The gap between these piles is where marketing lives.

Invented

Vendor benefit lists, 'neuroprotective' certainties, misattributed identities (see next chapter). No paper, no data — copy written to sell vials.

Plain English

Audit result: a thimble of verified science, a cup of plausible animal hints, and an ocean of invented marketing. The honest move with PE-22-28 isn't to hype or to dismiss the molecule — it's to refuse to fill the empty piles with wishes.

05The information fog

The information fog

No verified anecdotes exist for PE-22-28 — inventing any would be dishonest. What does exist is a fog of conflicting vendor claims. Here are its shapes, each stamped accordingly.

Information-fog note — the pulsing dot here marks documented patterns in how this compound is sold and discussed: not anecdotes, not evidence of effects.

The identity shuffle

Some vendors describe PE-22-28 as a spadin fragment (correct); others call it a galanin fragment or confuse it with NAP — entirely different peptides. Pattern: when sellers can't agree what a compound is, nothing else they say about it is trustworthy.

Caveat: verifiable by comparing vendor pages — the confusion is documented.
Pattern: Identity confusion is the reddest of flags.

The copy-paste benefit list

Vendor pages list mood, neuroprotection, and cognitive benefits in identical phrasing across competing sites. Pattern: shared marketing copy, not shared data — nobody cites a human study because none exists.

Caveat: observable across vendor catalogs.
Pattern: Consensus of sellers is not evidence.

The borrowed halo

Marketing leans on spadin's animal research as if it were PE-22-28's human record — parent's résumé, child's product page. Pattern: the classic borrowed-legitimacy move.

Caveat: a framing trick, not a data point.
Pattern: The parent's data is not the fragment's data.

The absent anecdotes

Search the forums: no sustained first-hand report culture exists for PE-22-28 — no trip reports, no logs, no community wisdom. Pattern: the silence is itself information about how marginal this compound is.

Caveat: an honest observation, not an anecdote.
Pattern: No anecdotes is also data — about obscurity.

The sequence shell game

Different sellers have listed different sequences and CAS numbers for 'PE-22-28' over the years. Pattern: with no standard and no testing culture, the name is a suggestion, not an identity.

Caveat: documented across catalog snapshots.
Pattern: A name without a verified structure is a rumor.

The review mirage

Five-star vendor reviews praise shipping speed and customer service — never measured effects. Pattern: in the absence of outcomes, logistics becomes the review.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

With obscure compounds, the risk file is almost entirely unknown — which is itself the risk. Here's the honest inventory.

Unknown

Human safety: blank

Unknown: no human trials means no safety data — not 'safe,' not 'dangerous,' but blank. Anyone claiming either is inventing it.

Known

Identity risk

Documented: vendors have misidentified this compound as other peptides. With obscure catalog items, you may not receive what the label says — and there's no easy way to check.

Theoretical

Brain-channel unknowns

Theoretical: TREK-1 channels regulate neuronal excitability broadly. Chronically blocking them in a human brain is unmapped territory — the mechanism cuts both ways.

Unknown

Interactions

Unknown: no interaction studies with any medication, including antidepressants — the very drugs a buyer might combine it with. Blank pages.

Unknown

Long-term effects

Unknown: no chronic-use data in any species relevant to human safety. The neurogenesis story is appealing; the long-term ledger is empty.

Known

The fog is the hazard

Observed: obscurity plus marketing creates false confidence — confident benefit lists with no data behind them, bought by people who mistake detail for evidence.

Plain English

Known: the name on the vial might be wrong, and the marketing is invented. Unknown: everything about what it does in a human. Obscurity isn't a safety profile — it's the absence of one.
